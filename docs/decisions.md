# Decisions

Format: what we decided, why, and the trade-off. Newest first.

## 2026-10-19: Statistics and the low reply-rate alert (advice, never a pause)
- **Decision:** `stats` (and `/stats` in Telegram) report, for the last 7 days and all time: companies emailed, first emails and follow-ups, replies with a rate (one reply per person, their first), positive replies, bounces, opt-outs and complaints, then one honest sentence on what the all-time numbers mean. After 300 companies emailed, a reply rate under 1% sends a Telegram alert (again only after 100 more emails). The alert is advice: it never pauses sending, unlike bounces and complaints, which do.
- **Why:** without reply rates the owner cannot tell whether the offer works, which matters more than any other feature. Under 1% after 300 emails means the offer or targeting is wrong and more volume makes it worse.
- **Limits:** meetings booked are not counted because the system never sees the calendar; the 3 to 5% benchmark is a vendor-reported range, not a promise.

## 2026-10-19: Telegram never shows more than 10 cards at once; follow-ups get short cards
- **Decision:** `MAX_OPEN_CARDS` (default 10) caps cards waiting for your review. Drafts beyond it wait in the database (status `draft`, lead `drafted`) and are released, follow-ups first, whenever you clear a card: after every run, by the bot as soon as you tap, and by the worker. A card waiting for your typed feedback counts as open. Follow-up cards are short (step, what the previous email was, the new text, the evidence it cites) because the signature and full evidence were already seen on email 1. `/queue` shows open and queued counts. Anyone who opts out while queued is dropped, never shown.
- **Why:** with four steps per lead the cards would otherwise pile up faster than they can be read. A separate chat only splits the pile.
- **Trade-offs:** drafts that wait in the queue are not refreshed by `refresh`, so they carry the rules of the day they were written; the send policy re-checks them again at send time. If you leave Telegram for days, cards wait; the daily send cap means a short delay costs almost nothing.

## 2026-10-19: Follow-up sequence: four emails, each approved by you, each with its own rules
- **Decision:** `icp.yaml` `sequence:` defines steps 2 to 4 (new angle after 3 working days, referral question after 5 more, breakup after 9 more, counted on the lead's own calendar). Each step has a kind with its own platform guardrails: new angle needs evidence and ends with a question; referral needs no evidence, may use only the honest proof wording, ends with a question; breakup offers "reply 1/2/3" and needs no question. All kinds keep the universal rules (no links, no urgency, no prices, no call requests, no client names; names come from `never_name` in icp.yaml, not from code). A follow-up is drafted only while the lead is `in_sequence`, so a reply, bounce, opt-out or an open approval card all stop or hold the sequence; an out-of-office notice holds it a week. Follow-ups go out as replies in the first email's thread and every one needs your approval while autonomy is level 0.
- **Safety rules found while building:** rejecting a follow-up returns the lead to `in_sequence` (it must never restart at step 1 and email someone twice); a reply or bounce withdraws every unsent email to that person; the send policy also refuses to send to anyone whose status says the sequence is over, even if an approved email is already queued; a first email is never drafted for someone already emailed.
- **Trade-offs:** three more approvals per lead means more taps for you; at about 15 sends a day that is manageable, and autonomy level 1 (follow-ups auto-send after 100 approved emails) is the planned relief. After the last email the lead is parked as `nurture` and never contacted again by the system.

## 2026-10-19: Company-style signature, sent as plain text plus a styled copy of the same words
- **Decision:** the signature follows the company's mail layout (bold name | title, italic company, Email | Office | Website, small-print confidentiality notice) from `.env` (`SENDER_*`). Each email is multipart: the approved plain text and a styled HTML copy. A test proves the HTML says exactly the same words as the plain text, and the worker sends the styled copy only if it matches what the owner approved (otherwise plain only). The HTML never has images or tracking, and model text is escaped. Opt-out line and postal address stay under the signature (law and platform rule).
- **Left out on purpose:** the logo image. Images in cold email raise spam scores and the logo adds a hosted or embedded picture to every first touch. Revisit after a few weeks of clean sending with replies.
- **Trade-off:** two links (mailto and website) appear in the styled signature; the first-touch rule against links covers the model's body text, which is still enforced.

## 2026-10-19: Inbox handling reads only mail about our emails, and starts from now
- **Decision:** `sender/inbox.py` polls the sending mailbox read-only over IMAP (nothing deleted, moved or flagged). The first run records the newest message and never reads history. A mail is acted on only if it references one of our Message-IDs, is a delivery failure naming one, or comes from an address we emailed; everything else is counted and skipped without being stored, logged or shown. Hard bounce: suppress the address, mark the lead, alert. Clear opt-out (keywords, or the model says unsubscribe/not_interested): suppress at once and tell the owner. Spam complaint: suppress and pause all sending. Any other reply: stop the sequence, save the text, alert with the text for a human to answer. Two hard bounces in the last 100 sends (or over 2% from 100 sends) pause sending automatically; only a human resumes. If the mailbox cannot be read, the worker skips sending that cycle.
- **Why:** the mailbox is the CEO's real inbox, so restraint is the privacy rule; bounces and complaints are what damage a main domain.
- **Trade-offs:** the reply label from the model is a convenience (a failed call never blocks the alert). Bounce parsing is tested on standard delivery reports; a real one from GoDaddy or Gmail should be checked once with a deliberate bad address on our own domain.

## 2026-10-19: Phase 2 sending: one gate, staged rollout, never send twice
- **Decision:** all sending lives in `zypher_agent/sender/` (a test fails if mail code appears anywhere else). Every message must pass the pure `decide()` policy at send time: approved for this exact version, not paused, not opted out, guardrails re-run, one first email per company, lead-local business hours, warmup-ramped daily cap (5 a day rising to 15, hard ceiling 20, owner may only lower it), spacing of 4 to 12 minutes. A message is claimed atomically before sending. Master switch `SENDING_ENABLED` is off by default; `SEND_ALLOWLIST` limits recipients during the rollout.
- **Why:** sending is irreversible and we are on the company's main domain; the plan in docs/phase2-plan.md was approved by the owner. DNS check found mail is hosted at GoDaddy (SPF/DMARC already correct), not Google.
- **Trade-offs:** a connection that dies mid-send is marked failed and never retried (a duplicate cold email is worse than a missed one; the owner is alerted to check the Sent folder). Bounce and reply detection (inbox reading) is not built yet, so until it is, early volume must stay tiny and the owner watches the mailbox by hand.

## 2026-10-19: Evidence-specific drafts, better inbox choice, Reject only discards the draft
- **Decision:** (1) a draft must reuse at least two concrete words or numbers from the quotes it cites, else it fails `specific_detail` and Approve is disabled; (2) inbox choice ranks decision makers, then sales/enquiries, then info/contact, and support desks last; (3) Reject sets the lead back to `researched` so a new draft is written, instead of disqualifying the company; (4) research prefers quotes about how the business works and with numbers, never addresses or slogans.
- **Why:** seen live: three of five drafts were the same email for every freight company (generic "free quote" pitch), and two went to support desks. Reject permanently killing a lead after one weak draft threw away paid-for leads.
- **Trade-off:** leads whose websites carry no concrete evidence now produce a failed card instead of a sendable weak email. That is intended: fewer, better emails. The word-overlap test is a proxy for "specific"; the owner's approval remains the final judge.

## 2026-10-19: Leads come from Google Maps via Apify; emails only from the company's own site
- **Decision:** `zypher_agent source` runs Apify's `compass/crawler-google-places` (places with a website, open only) and imports companies with no email. During research the pipeline takes the best public business address from their own pages (own domain first; careers/no-reply/HR inboxes skipped), checks the domain accepts mail, and checks the do-not-contact list. No usable address: status `nurture`, phone kept for a human.
- **Why:** automation instead of hand-built spreadsheets; public business addresses are the defensible source for B2B outreach; we do not buy Apify's contact enrichment (personal data, extra cost).
- **Trade-offs:** Maps categories are noisy ("general trading company" also returns shops and travel agents); the research agent's disqualifiers and your approval filter them. Sites on shared hosts (netlify.app, base44.app and similar) are skipped because the domain cannot identify the company. Measured live: 30 Dubai companies for about $0.13; 3 of 3 researched leads had a usable public email.

## 2026-10-19: Supabase Data API is locked (RLS on, no policies)
- **Decision:** migration 003 enables row-level security on every table with no policies.
- **Why:** Supabase exposes all `public` tables through its REST API, and the `anon` key is meant to be public. Without RLS, anyone with that key could read or change leads. The agent connects as the table owner, which RLS does not apply to.
- **Trade-off:** none for the agent. When we later add a UI or per-tenant access through the API, we write real policies then.

## 2026-10-19: First touch is question-first and pitches one offer (automation)
- **Decision:** `icp.yaml` now lists three offers (automation, software, branding). Only the one marked `first_touch` is pitched in email 1; the others wait for the discovery call. First touch is 50 to 80 words, ends with a yes/no question offering a one-page sketch ("no call needed"), and must not contain a call request, links or urgency. Prompts and the own-name filter are built from config (no business name in code).
- **Why:** Saad's offer-first method and the cold-email research: a hyper-specific single promise and a tiny ask get replies; three vague services or a meeting request in email 1 do not. Websites and branding are crowded commodity markets where the cheapest wins, so they are follow-ups, not openers.
- **Trade-offs:** a small test set of regexes (call request, links, urgency) can mis-flag odd wording ("a phone call" in an observation); the retry loop and your approval catch that. Offer wording in `icp.yaml` is a draft the owner must confirm. Pricing and the USR proof details are still undecided.

## 2026-10-18: LLM provider is configurable; MVP runs on free open-weight models via Groq
- **Decision:** All LLM calls go through the `LLM` interface. `LLM_PROVIDER=openai_compat` (default) talks to any OpenAI-compatible API (Groq, Ollama, Together, Mistral, NIM). Claude stays available (`anthropic`). Default models: `openai/gpt-oss-120b`, falling back to `openai/gpt-oss-20b`.
- **Why:** The owner cannot pay for an API yet, a local model cannot be given to partners or demo clients, and the product must be able to switch provider later without a rewrite. Groq's free tier supports strict JSON-schema output on both models. **Measured live (2026-10-19):** free tier allows 8K tokens per minute per request on gpt-oss, so research input is capped at about 3.5K tokens (`PROMPT_TEXT_BUDGET`); Qwen 3.8 allows only 1K output tokens per minute and burned 1K on hidden reasoning for one short email, so it was dropped as the fallback.
- **How quality is protected:** strict schema output, Pydantic validation with one corrective retry, a model chain on rate limits, the verified-quote check in research, the guardrails, and human approval. `scripts/eval_models.py` compares models on real leads (guardrail pass rate, verified hypotheses, blind-rated drafts) so the default is chosen by measurement.
- **Trade-offs:** free tier has no SLA, a 200K tokens/day cap per model (about 14 leads/day per model), and unstated data-handling terms. Not a permanent production foundation: move to a paid tier or a paid host before partners depend on it. Free-tier calls are logged without a cost (no price known).

## 2026-10-18: Cold email is sent from the main domain (owner's decision, against advice)
- **Decision:** Sender was `info@zypher-solutions.com`; changed on 2026-10-19 to the founder's own address `muhammedhasheem@zypher-solutions.com` (founder-led outreach: owners reply to owners). Still the main domain.
- **Advice given:** use a separate domain and a personal address. Spam complaints and bounces damage the domain that carries the company's real mail, and recovery takes weeks.
- **Mitigations to build in Phase 2 (sender module, not tenant-configurable):** hard daily cap (start at 10/day), automatic pause if bounce rate passes 2% or any spam complaint arrives, SPF/DKIM/DMARC verified before first send, warmup ramp, plain-text first touch with no links.
- **Revisit:** move to a separate domain before volume grows or if any deliverability warning appears. The sender address is configuration, so switching needs no code change.

## 2026-10-17: Tenancy is enforced in the repository layer, suppression is per tenant
- **Decision:** `tenant_id` on every table. A repository is bound to one tenant at construction and scopes every query. Other tenants' ids behave as nonexistent. Suppression and domain uniqueness are per tenant.
- **Why:** hard to forget (no per-call argument), and no auth or row-level security is needed yet.
- **Trade-off:** a global list for hard bounces and spam complaints (useful for deliverability) is deferred to the sender module in Phase 2. A bug in the repository layer would leak across tenants, so isolation has contract tests that run against both implementations.
